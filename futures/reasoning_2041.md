# Reasoning before the 2041 election (after 2038)

> **Note (official-count rerun, 27 November 2026):** these futures now start from the official 2026 count; they first started from the preliminary count. The reasoning rules are unchanged, and follow roles (left leader, right partners, …) rather than party names, so they keep working if, say, the Greens overtake Labour. The figures below come from this rerun and match the digests in this folder.

## What 2038 showed

- **MMP**: overhangs still 4–23 seats (median 12; Houses up to 144). In world 5
  the left won 54% of the vote but only drew level, 69–69 in a 138-seat House,
  because of a 17-seat overhang (National 10, TPM 7).
- **MMP-T / MMP-BV**: overhang fell to a median of 3–4. Where Green and
  Opportunity voters consolidated behind Labour in marginals, National's
  plurality overhang disappeared (MMP-T: world 0, 11 → 0; world 5, 12 → 0;
  world 9, 10 → 0). It persisted where National is very strong and the left is
  fragmented (worlds 2 and 3: 11–15 seats, still absorbed). In left landslides
  Labour itself now overhangs by a seat (worlds 5, 7 and 9), and the mirror case
  appears for ACT and NZ First voters. TPM's party vote is now 4–11%, so its
  overhang is mostly gone. In world 8 the right still won a majority, 63 of
  121, on 49.6% of the vote.

## How voters respond

Incentives are unchanged from 2038, and the voters who saw consolidation work
keep doing it: the same rules are applied again from the new starting point
(consolidation in marginals where the other side's leader overhangs, up to a
split rate of 0.35; left personal splitting keeps easing; TPM closes its gap
where it still overhangs). Under MMP the arms race continues as before.
