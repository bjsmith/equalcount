# Reasoning before the 2038 election (after 2035)

## What 2035 showed

- **MMP**: overhangs grew (median 17 seats; Houses 127–143). National
  overhung by 8–16 in most right-leaning worlds; TPM still won 3–7 Māori seats
  on about 1% of the party vote.
- **MMP-T / MMP-BV**: ACT and NZ First voters now rarely split (0.06–0.2), and
  TPM's party vote reached 2.9–5.8%, so TPM's overhang shrank to 0–3. But
  **National still overhung by 3–15 seats in right-leaning worlds**, almost all
  from winning electorates on plurality against a split left. With fewer split
  ballots to transfer, that overhang was absorbed in most of those worlds, and
  every other party paid in list seats. Median bloc deficit under the reforms
  (3.4–4.0) is now *worse* than under MMP (2.8). This is the failure mode we
  found at the start: the reform removes the reward for splitting, and what
  is left is plurality overhang.

## How voters respond

**MMP**: unchanged incentives; the general rules continue, with continued
pressure on Green voters to tick Labour where National's overhang is large.

**MMP-T / MMP-BV**: the incentive has flipped for left voters.
- Splitting *to* a winning candidate from another party costs your party.
  But backing *your own side's* strongest candidate where National would
  otherwise win costs nothing (Labour rarely overhangs in these worlds), and
  it shrinks the overhang that is costing the left list seats.
- So engaged Green and Opportunity voters in marginal seats begin to tick
  Labour: their split rate roughly doubles plus 5 points (cap 0.35). This is
  partial: many voters won't follow the reasoning.
- The mirror case applies where Labour overhangs (ACT and NZ First voters).
- Left voters' personal splitting for National MPs keeps falling (×0.9).
- TPM keeps closing the gap to ~4% where it still overhangs.
