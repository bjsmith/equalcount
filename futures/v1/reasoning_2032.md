# Reasoning before the 2032 election (after 2029)

## What 2029 showed

- **Under MMP** every world had an overhang of 11–17 seats, a 132–136 seat House,
  and the same two engines as 2026: National winning far more electorates than
  its party vote (ACT and NZ First voters ticking National candidates), and
  Te Pāti Māori winning 5–7 Māori seats on well under 1% of the party vote
  (Labour and Green voters ticking TPM candidates; TPM's own supporters
  party-voting Green or Labour).
- **Under MMP-T and MMP-BV** (first election under each) voters had barely
  changed behaviour (awareness 0.2), so the same overhangs had to be
  transferred: 55,000–220,000 party votes moved. ACT and NZ First each lost
  2–5 seats where National overhung; Labour and Green lost 2–4 each (some
  through their voters' personal votes for National MPs, most through the TPM
  transfer). TPM's overhang could not be covered even with every eligible
  ballot, so it was absorbed: the left paid for TPM's seats in list seats.

## How each group of voters responds

**MMP worlds.** Splitting works and nothing punishes it.
- *ACT and NZ First voters* keep ticking National candidates; a little more
  each time National overhangs (general rule, +0.03).
- *Green voters*: where the right won more seats than votes and the majority,
  left strategists run "tick Labour in marginals" campaigns; Green voters'
  split rate rises by 40%. Not fully rational — many Greens still tick Green.
- *National voters*: where the left won the majority on an overhang, National
  copies the cup-of-tea approach more openly: about a point of National's
  vote goes to ACT or NZ First's list while those voters keep ticking the
  National candidate.
- *Māori seats*: the TPM strategy paid again; it continues.

**MMP-T and MMP-BV worlds.** The 2029 transfers were large and public.
- *ACT and NZ First* lost seats because their voters ticked National
  candidates. Both parties run "two ticks" campaigns. Their voters are
  engaged, so the response is fast: split rates fall 45% (30% where they lost
  fewer than 3 seats), on top of the general learning rule.
- *Green and Opportunity voters* respond more slowly (25% cut): their splits
  are more often personal (a liked local MP) than tactical.
- *Te Pāti Māori*: its electorates no longer bring the left extra seats and
  the left pays for them. TPM switches to asking for the party vote. That's
  party-led, so faster than ordinary voter learning: it recovers about half
  the gap to its 2023 party vote (3.1%), taken from Green and Labour.
- *Labour and Green voters in Māori seats* partly return to two ticks
  (s_M 0.40 → 0.32).
- *MMP-BV only*: the backup vote is starting to be understood (awareness 0.5
  after a visible first election): sub-threshold parties' voters fear wasting
  their vote less, and pick a qualifying candidate for their electorate vote.
