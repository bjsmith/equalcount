# Reasoning before the 2035 election (after 2032)

## What 2032 showed

- **MMP**: overhangs of 4–26 seats; Houses of 126–147. Te Pāti Māori kept
  winning 4–7 Māori seats on under 1–1.6% of the party vote. In world 9
  National overhung by 19 (147-seat House). No world produced a majority for
  the bloc with fewer votes, but seat shares ran well ahead of vote shares.
- **MMP-T / MMP-BV**: ACT and NZ First voters split far less (split rates
  0.14–0.31, from 0.35), so their seat losses fell. But National still
  overhung by 1–14 seats in most right-leaning worlds: that overhang now
  comes mainly from **winning electorates on plurality**, not from partners'
  splitting. The pool of split ballots is thinner, so National's overhang was
  absorbed in three worlds, and **Labour lost 2–6 seats** because some Labour
  voters ticked a National MP they liked. TPM's party vote rose to 1.8–4.7%
  and its overhang shrank to 1–4, but was still absorbed in most worlds.

## How voters respond

**MMP**: the arms race goes on. Where National's overhang was 8 or more,
Labour pushes Green voters in marginal seats harder to tick Labour (Green
split rate ×1.3). Otherwise the general rules apply (partners' splitting edges
up while their leader overhangs; the TPM strategy continues).

**MMP-T / MMP-BV**:
- *Labour and Green voters* have now seen twice that ticking a local National
  MP or a TPM candidate costs their own party list seats. Their parties
  campaign on it. Personal splitting falls by 15% (floor 0.12): many voters
  still back a local MP they like regardless.
- *ACT and NZ First*: where they still lost 2+ seats, their two-tick campaigns
  intensify (split rate ×0.7).
- *Te Pāti Māori* keeps rebuilding its party vote. Holding 4–6 electorates
  needs about 3–4.5% at 121 seats, so it aims for ~4% and closes half the gap.
- *Labour/Green voters in Māori seats*: a few more return to two ticks
  (s_M −0.03, floor 0.25).
