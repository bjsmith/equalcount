# Reasoning before the 2038 election (after 2035)

> **Note (official-count rerun, 27 November 2026):** these futures now start from the official 2026 count; they first started from the preliminary count. The reasoning rules are unchanged, and follow roles (left leader, right partners, …) rather than party names, so they keep working if, say, the Greens overtake Labour. The figures below come from this rerun and match the digests in this folder.

## What 2035 showed

- **MMP**: overhangs grew (median 15 seats; Houses 126–144). National
  overhung by 4–17 in right-leaning worlds; TPM still won 3–7 Māori seats
  on 0.1–1.3% of the party vote. In world 5 the right won a majority, 78 of
  141, on 49.2% of the vote, carried by National's 13-seat overhang.
- **MMP-T / MMP-BV**: ACT and NZ First voters now split far less (0.06–0.28),
  and TPM's party vote reached 2.9–5.8%, so TPM's overhang shrank to 0–3. But
  **National still overhung by 3–15 seats in right-leaning worlds**, almost all
  from winning electorates on plurality against a split left. With fewer split
  ballots to transfer, that overhang was absorbed in 13 of the 20 reform
  worlds, and every other party paid in list seats. Median bloc deficit under
  the reforms (MMP-BV 4.2, MMP-T 4.5) is now *worse* than under MMP (2.8), and
  in worlds 1 and 5 (both reforms) the right won a majority with fewer votes.
  This is the failure mode we found at the start: the reform removes the
  reward for splitting, and what is left is plurality overhang.

## How voters respond

**MMP**: unchanged incentives; the general rules continue, with continued
pressure on Green voters to tick Labour where National's overhang is large.

**MMP-T / MMP-BV**: the incentive has flipped for left voters.
- Splitting *to* a winning candidate from another party costs your party.
  But backing *your own side's* strongest candidate where National would
  otherwise win costs nothing (Labour rarely overhangs in these worlds), and
  it shrinks the overhang that is costing the left list seats.
- So engaged Green and Opportunity voters in marginal seats begin to tick
  Labour: their split rate roughly doubles plus 5 points (cap 0.35). This is
  partial: many voters won't follow the reasoning.
- The mirror case applies where Labour overhangs (ACT and NZ First voters).
- Left voters' personal splitting for National MPs keeps falling (×0.9).
- TPM keeps closing the gap to ~4% where it still overhangs.
